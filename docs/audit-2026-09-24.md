# Audit, 24 Sep 2026: pace, race class, head-to-head, speed

Four things checked at the owner's request after the 23 Sep Happy Valley
meeting. Each section says what was wrong, how it was measured, and what
changed. The production steps are at the end.

---

## 1. Race pace was read against the wrong reference

**What it did.** A race's pace was the average first-section time of its
runners, as a z-score against every race in the archive at the same distance.
That pools:

- **both courses and the all-weather.** Sha Tin's 1000m is a straight course.
  HKJC's standard for its first 200m is 13.10s, against 12.50s at Happy Valley.
- **every class.** At Sha Tin 1200m the standard first 400m is 23.45s for a
  Group race and 23.90s for Class 5.
- **every going.** A yielding day made every race "Slow".

It also read only the first section, which at 1000, 1400, 1800 and 2200m is
the 200m jump.

**23 Sep 2026, against HKJC's own standards:**

| Race | Old reading | Leader to the 800m vs the class standard | New reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 1650 C5 | Neutral | 0.51s faster (day-adjusted) | Fast |
| R4 1200 C4 | **Fast** | 0.11s slower | Neutral |
| R5 1650 C4 | Neutral | 0.78s faster | Fast |
| R6 1000 C4 | **Fast** | on standard | Neutral |
| R7 1200 C3 | Neutral | 0.33s slower | Slow |
| R8 1200 C3 | Fast | on standard | Neutral |
| R9 1800 C3 | Fast | 0.89s faster | Fast |

**What it does now** (`derive/tempo`, table `race_tempo`):

1. **The leader's splits.** It takes the leader's time at each of HKJC's
   section marks, which is how HKJC builds its own race sectional.
2. **The early reading runs to the 800m-to-go mark.** At 1000m it runs to the
   400m instead, because the first section there is only the 200m jump.
3. **It is read against HKJC's reference sectionals** for that course,
   distance and class. They are scraped from HKJC's course standard times page
   by `ingest/standards` and refreshed weekly by the nightly job.
4. **The standard is scaled by the day's track.** The day's track is the
   median over that meeting's races of the winning time against its standard.
   This covers the going without guessing a going allowance.
5. **Every page prints the result in seconds with a band:** "Fast (−0.78s)".
   The tooltip names the standard it was read against.

**Why "to the 800m".** Tested on 4,865 races by how often the winner led at
the first call, from the fastest fifth of races to the slowest:

| Early reading | Fastest fifth | 2nd | 3rd | 4th | Slowest fifth |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| To the 800m (new) | 21.0% | 23.9% | 26.4% | 30.0% | 38.2% |
| To the 400m | 25.1% | 24.9% | 24.7% | 28.0% | 37.0% |
| Old z-score | 19.9% | 26.2% | 25.7% | 29.6% | 38.4% |

The 800m reading sorts races at every step. The old z-score wasn't useless,
but only 36% of races keep the same band under the new one.

**Lookup's RACE PACE filter was also inverted.** It banded each runner's own
early split against its own field, not the race's tempo, and a runner quicker
than its field read "Very Slow". It now filters on `race_tempo.band`.

**ET is not affected.** ET's par is the median finish time of every runner in
the cell, so a typical winner rates about 0.65s (4-5 lengths) better than par.
That is by design, and the page says "vs par". The archive's winning times
match HKJC's standards to about 0.1s on the median, which also checks the
finish-time data.

## 2. Race class: one vocabulary, read from every way HKJC writes it

**What was wrong.**

- **The race card read the site's navigation menu.** It took the first
  "Group …" anywhere on the page, and the menu carries "Group One" above the
  race header. 27 Sep R8, a Group 3, was stored as a Group 1.
- **The results parser read one phrasing of five.** HKJC writes a class as
  "Class 4", "Class 3 (Restricted)", "Group Three", "Griffin Race" or
  "4 Year Olds". The parser accepted only the first, so every Group race,
  every restricted race and the whole 4-year-old series (Classic Mile, Classic
  Cup, Derby) arrived with no class: 196 races.
- **Legacy group races.** 70 more held the old archive's "0" for a Group race
  of unknown grade.

**What it does now.** `store/coerce.to_race_class` writes one vocabulary:
`1`–`5`, `G1`–`G3`, `Listed`, `Griffin Race` and `4YO`. A new column,
`races.restricted`, flags a narrower field: HKJC's "(Restricted)" races
(usually 3- or 4-year-olds), the 4YO series and Griffin races. HKJC's header
does not say which age a "(Restricted)" race is for, so the flag says
restricted and no more. The card parser reads only the race's own header
block. Pages show "C3 (R)", "G3", "4YO" and "GRIF".

**Repair.** `python -m hkrd.jobs.repair --fix --only classes` reads HKJC's
all-results page, which carries one line per race for the whole meeting. That
is one request per meeting, 610 meetings, about 12 minutes.

On the scratch copy the first pass changed 264 classes. It also exposed a
flaw in its own header reader: the last race of each meeting never matched,
because the pattern needed the text after it to run to the end of the page
and a newline stopped it. The reader now matches each header on its own, and
the test reproduces the old failure. A second pass read those 518 last races:
their classes were already right, and it set their restricted flag.

Three races are left with no class. Two are rows on 15 Jul 2026 with no
header at all. The third is the 2019 Griffin Trophy, which HKJC headed
"Restricted Race", a sixth phrasing that appears once in the archive and not
since. It is left alone rather than guessed.

## 3. Head-to-head: margin, draw and rider, never the weight

Over 91,856 pairs meeting again within a year:

- The earlier order repeats **58%** of the time.
- A margin of a length or less repeats **51%**; 8L or more, **68%**.
- When the beaten horse's rider is 5+ points of strike rate better
  relatively, the repeat falls to **50%**, where form alone expects 56%.
- The weight swing points the wrong way. With the beaten horse now 8lb+
  *worse* off, the repeat is **49%**; with it 8lb+ better off, **61–62%**.
  The weight follows the rating, so the swing mostly shows which horse has
  been winning since.

The Race Day band is now sorted by how open the rematch is. Each pair names
the horse that was beaten, by how much, and how often margins that close
repeat. A green mark appears when the draw or the rider has swung its way.
The weight gap is still shown, labelled "follows the rating", with no badge
and no sort. `query/h2h` builds the pairs from one read of the field's
history, where the old code made one query per pair (66–91 per card).

## 4. Speed

Measured in-process on a copy the size of production (5,842 races, 71,400
runners). Each figure is warm, with the first call in brackets:

| Endpoint | Time |
|---|---|
| Screen, whole card | 121 ms (446 ms) |
| One Race Day card | 21–28 ms |
| The Briefing's 11 race cards | 445 ms in sequence (parallel in the browser) |
| Form Guide | 139 ms |
| Lookup insight | 207 ms (364 ms) |
| Tips summary, money, meeting strip | under 20 ms each |

**The Screen doesn't slow the rest of the dashboard.** It is one request, sent
in parallel with the rest and drawn on its own, so a slow or failed Screen
never holds up the board below it. The rider's year, the one aggregate over a
year of runners, is now cached for ten minutes per date. It is identical for
every race on a card, and Race Day was computing it once per race.

**The nightly job now derives only what new results can move.** It used to
rebuild the whole archive whenever anything landed, several times on a race
day, on the same single shared CPU that serves the dashboard. Timings on the
production-size copy:

| Step, whole archive | Time |
|---|---|
| pace | 1.2 s |
| tempo | 0.5 s |
| ET | 3.7 s |
| **SARR** | **87.5 s** |
| tags | 9.6 s |

SARR is walk-forward: a race's rating reads only the runs before it. New
results can therefore move only the races after them, and upcoming cards are
rescored separately. `derive_all.run_landed` now does the following:

- **Pace and tempo** run for the meetings that landed.
- **SARR** runs for every settled meeting from the earliest landed date on,
  usually just the one. A late meeting landing after a later one was scored
  still reaches it.
- **ET and tags** run over the whole archive, as before (13 s).

Measured on the production-size copy for one landed meeting (23 Sep): 19 s, against about 100 s for the whole-archive rebuild.

**Worth doing next, not done here:** the Briefing fetches 11 full Race Day
cards only to draw its signal chips. A `lite` card, without the odds
sparkline, money, pair money and doubles, or one meeting-level signals call,
would cut that to about a third. On Fly's single shared CPU the 11 cards are
the Briefing's slowest part.

## Production steps

The `briefing` branch is not deployed. When it is, run these in order:

1. `python -m hkrd.jobs.scrape_standards`: one request. The nightly job also
   does this on its own within a week.
2. `python -m hkrd.jobs.repair --fix --only classes`: about 12 minutes.
3. `python -m hkrd.jobs.rebuild_tempo`: about a second.
4. The next nightly scrape of the 27 Sep card writes R8 as G3. Running
   `python -m hkrd.jobs.scrape_meeting --date 2026-09-27 --venue ST` does it
   now.
